## The period accent (shared by every character except the Narrator)

**What it's built on.** Akkadian (the language of Zik, Bel-iddin, Kurash and the Collector's
world) and Sumerian (the older temple-and-archive language of Uruk) are known only from cuneiform,
so every reconstruction is a scholarly best guess. What scholars agree on:
- **Pure vowels only:** a, e, i, u. Akkadian had **no "o" sound**. Vowels are held clean, never
  glided into diphthongs ("ay", "oh").
- **Vowel length mattered:** long vowels were held noticeably longer.
- **š = "sh", ṣ = "ts", ḫ = a throaty "kh"** as in Scottish *loch* (Enhedu's name has it).
- **Doubled consonants were held:** Aššur is "Ahsh-shoor", Ammaru is "Ahm-mah-roo".
- **Emphatic consonants:** t, s and q were pronounced deep and "dark" in the throat.
- **Sumerian also had "ŋ"**, the "ng" in *sing*, used at the start of syllables.

**How to use it.** Every prompt below includes the same accent sentence, so the whole cast sounds
like one civilisation:

> *a faint, consistent ancient Mesopotamian accent modeled on reconstructed Akkadian: pure open vowels (ah, eh, ee, oo) with no diphthongs, a lightly tapped R, crisp held double consonants, a soft throaty 'kh', and a deliberate, formal cadence — not Arabic, not Persian, not modern*

Keep it **faint**. The story is in English; the accent is texture, not caricature. If ElevenLabs
overdoes it on a character, cut the accent sentence down to *"faint ancient Mesopotamian accent:
pure vowels, tapped R"* and regenerate.

**Speaking style of the period.** Akkadian and Sumerian speech as it survives in letters, laws
and epics is formal and weighted: names come with lineage ("Bel-iddin, son of Naram"),
statements are blunt and final, oaths and warnings are slow and deliberate. The prompts build
that into delivery: unhurried, sentences that land with a firm ending, very little modern
upward inflection.

## Zik (Zikir Ashur)

Lead archaeologist, early-to-mid 30s. Dry, skeptical, precise; deflects with logic, says "Nothing"
when it's something. Grief for his father sits under everything. Weakens badly at the end.

**Prompt:** A man in his early 30s, an archaeologist and tomb-robber of ancient Mesopotamia, with a mid-low, dry, quietly confident voice and a faint, consistent ancient Mesopotamian accent modeled on reconstructed Akkadian: pure open vowels with no diphthongs, a lightly tapped R, crisp held double consonants, a soft throaty 'kh', and a deliberate, formal cadence — not Arabic, not Persian, not modern. Skeptical and economical with words; deadpan humor delivered completely flat; explains technical things as if they are obvious. Calm and guarded, with old grief buried under the dryness. Natural pacing. Studio-quality recording, clean, no background noise.

**Preview:** Clay melts if you get it hot enough. Not campfire hot. Kiln hot. Hot enough that whoever built this either knew something we have forgotten, or they were trying to seal something badly enough that they invented a way.

## Balag

Veteran crewman, late 40s, huge. Plain words, complaint as affection, profanity under stress,
grandfather's wards and gate-stories. Deeply loyal.

**Prompt:** A very large, weathered laborer of ancient Uruk in his late 40s with a deep, heavy, grounded chest voice and a faint, consistent ancient Mesopotamian accent modeled on reconstructed Akkadian: broad pure vowels, a rolled R, heavy held consonants and a soft throaty 'kh' — not Arabic, not Persian, not modern. Slow, plain, economical speech; dry humor he never performs; grumbling that is really affection; when he speaks of his grandfather's old warnings his voice drops and turns grave. Unshakable, then genuinely frightened only when his memory fails. Studio-quality recording, clean, no background noise.

**Preview:** My grandfather told stories about seven gates. First gate took a man's shadow. Second took the voice people knew him by. Third took the hand he trusted. And the fourth took his name.

## Enhedu

Crew member, early-mid 30s. Quick, street-sharp, prices everything, swears. After Chapter 29
her ability to care is taken: same voice, flattened.

**Prompt:** A sharp, quick woman in her early 30s from the back streets of ancient Uruk, a dealer in what others leave behind, with a crisp, low-mid, slightly husky voice and a faint, consistent ancient Mesopotamian accent modeled on reconstructed Akkadian: clipped pure vowels, a tapped R, crisp consonants and a soft throaty 'kh' — not Arabic, not Persian, not modern. Dry, amused, quick-witted and street-smart; weighs the value of everything she hears; teasing lines are understated, warnings are cool and certain. Never bubbly. Studio-quality recording, clean, no background noise.

**Preview:** You two bicker like a married pair. Everything has a price. Some prices are better left unpaid. Whatever is inside wasn't trying to leave.

## Ninsun (Ninsun Etana)

Former university scholar of the lower archives, late 20s. Formal, few contractions, precise,
pedantic when scared. Lost her sister to the City. She is the one who walks out.

**Prompt:** A slight, intense woman in her late 20s, a scribe-scholar of the temple archives of ancient Uruk, with a clear, precise, mid-pitched voice and a faint, consistent ancient Mesopotamian accent modeled on reconstructed Sumerian and Akkadian scribal speech: pure vowels, carefully articulated consonants, held double consonants and an occasional soft 'ng', with the measured formality of someone who reads dead languages aloud — not Arabic, not Persian, not modern. Speaks formally, rarely uses contractions, dry academic wit; when frightened she becomes more exact, not louder. Grief for a lost sister burns underneath. Studio-quality recording, clean, no background noise.

**Preview:** I am not asking to be hired. I am telling you I already know more about the symbol on your skin than the Collector does, and I am coming with you whether you like the arrangement or not.

## Kurash (Chapter 1)

Youngest crewman, about 19. Eager, hungry for a better life, laughs too loud and too fast.

**Prompt:** A young man around 19, a hungry new hand on an ancient Mesopotamian dig crew, with a light, bright, eager voice and a faint, consistent ancient Mesopotamian accent modeled on reconstructed Akkadian: pure open vowels, a tapped R, crisp consonants — not Arabic, not Persian, not modern. Talks a little fast, laughs a little too loud, curious and impatient, dreaming of gold, a house and a better life. Earnest rather than foolish. Studio-quality recording, clean, no background noise.

**Preview:** My uncle always said the old kings hid their treasure because they feared thieves. So why bury it at all? That is impossible. It is unfair.

## Bel-iddin (Zik's father)

Surveyor, 60s, in memory, journal ink and one vision. Teacher; precise; grave; urgent when he
writes the second-door warning.

**Prompt:** An older man in his 60s, a surveyor and scholar of ancient Mesopotamia speaking to his son, with a low, warm, weathered voice and a faint, consistent ancient Mesopotamian accent modeled on reconstructed Akkadian: long, pure, held vowels, a tapped R, held double consonants and a soft throaty 'kh', with the slow formality of someone reciting a lesson — not Arabic, not Persian, not modern. A patient, precise teacher; grave and intimate; calm certainty, with deep sorrow underneath. Studio-quality recording, clean, no background noise.

**Preview:** Read the mortar, not the bricks. Bricks lie. Men shape them to look holy or expensive. Nobody bothers faking the mortar.

## The Collector

Antiquities dealer, late 50s, possibly not entirely human. Narrow, patient, faintly amused; never
raises his voice; sounds closer than he is.

**Prompt:** A refined, narrow man in his late 50s, a dealer in stolen antiquities in ancient Uruk, with a smooth, quiet, silky voice and a faint, consistent ancient Mesopotamian accent modeled on reconstructed Akkadian: long pure vowels, softly rolled R, precise held consonants, the polished speech of someone who deals with priests and nobles — not Arabic, not Persian, not modern. Patient, unhurried, faintly amused; courteous with a cold undertone; never raises his voice. Recorded very close to the microphone so he always sounds nearer than he is. Studio-quality recording, clean, no background noise.

**Preview:** The desert is famously responsible for the poor decisions of men. A man who claims to know everything is selling religion. I am selling time.

## The Dead King

Crowned skeleton, one line. Keep the Chatterbox reference performance if you prefer; this is the
ElevenLabs version.

**Prompt:** An ancient, dead king of a forgotten Mesopotamian dynasty speaking through dried, ruined vocal cords, with a brittle, scratchy, nearly whispered voice and the faint ghost of an archaic Akkadian accent: long held vowels, a dry rolled R, every consonant forced out with effort. Slow, unnervingly calm, quiet but absolutely certain. No growl, no monster roar. Studio-quality recording, clean, no background noise.

**Preview (test only, not canon):** Dust settles. Stone forgets. But I remember every name that was ever spoken in this room. Zikir Ashur.

## Mardu (lower-archive custodian, Ch. 16)

**Prompt:** An old archive custodian of ancient Uruk in his late 60s, stooped from thirty years over tablets, with a dry, steady, slightly papery voice and a faint, consistent ancient Mesopotamian accent modeled on reconstructed Sumerian and Akkadian scribal speech: pure vowels, precise consonants, the formal cadence of a temple bureaucrat — not Arabic, not Persian, not modern. Not frail; worn smooth by repetition. Bone-dry wit delivered with a completely straight face; quietly kind underneath. Studio-quality recording, clean, no background noise.

**Preview:** I have spent thirty-one years listening to people become quiet on the other side of it. I am old. Everything takes too long. Water dislikes bureaucracy.

## Zik's Mother (Ch. 5)

## The City (its own voice)

**Prompt:** An inhuman, wet, whispering voice that seems to come from deep stone and black water at once, with the faint archaic cadence of an ancient Sumerian lament: long pure vowels, a soft 'ng', words breathed rather than spoken. Slow, patient, intimate and wrong. Studio-quality recording, clean, no background noise.

**Preview (test only):** Ammaru. Ammaru. A name spoken is a door left open, and every door I have ever been given, I have kept.
