# The First City — ElevenLabs Voice Design Prompts

Paste each **Prompt** into ElevenLabs → Voices → Create a Voice → Voice Design, and the **Preview** line into the text box.
Generate the Narrator first and lock it; design everyone else to contrast with it. Save each voice with the exact character name shown here so the script's speaker labels match.

Script: `book-data/elevenlabs/scripts/` (one file per chapter, plus `THE_FIRST_CITY_FULL_SCRIPT.txt`).

**Tags in the script** (eleven_v3 audio tags): `[whispers]` `[softly]` `[shouts]` `[laughs]` `[sighs]` `[snorts]` `[mutters]` `[gasps]` `[sharply]` `[trembling]` `[urgently]` `[amused]` `[flatly]` `[weakly]` `[exhausted]` `[eerie]` `[slowly]`. `[whispers]`, `[shouts]`, `[laughs]` and `[sighs]` are confirmed by ElevenLabs; the rest are descriptive tags v3 usually honors — if one gets read aloud, delete it.

**Pauses** are on their own lines as `[pause 1.5s]`. In ElevenLabs Studio insert a pause there; when pasting straight into v3, replace the line with `…` or leave a blank paragraph.

## Narrator

**Prompt:** An audiobook narrator, a man in his late 40s with a low, warm, slightly dry and sun-worn voice and a neutral accent. Intelligent, restrained and observant, never theatrical. Measured pacing around 150 words per minute, close and intimate, letting dread build quietly through understatement. Suited to dark archaeological fantasy. Studio-quality recording, clean, no background noise.

**Preview:** The desert spat secrets when it felt like it. Today it gave up a ridge of brick half-buried in sand. A windstorm had stripped the dunes down to hard earth and left the upper edge exposed.

## Zik (Zikir Ashur)

**Prompt:** A man in his early 30s with a mid-low, dry, quietly confident voice and a neutral accent. Skeptical, precise and economical with words, with deadpan humor delivered completely flat. Calm and controlled, explaining technical things as if they are obvious, with a guarded weariness underneath. Natural conversational pacing. Studio-quality recording, clean, no background noise.

**Preview:** Clay melts if you get it hot enough. Not campfire hot. Kiln hot. Hot enough that whoever built this either knew something we've forgotten, or they were trying to seal something badly enough that they invented a way.

## Balag

**Prompt:** A very large man in his late 40s with a deep, heavy, grounded voice and a neutral accent. Slow, economical speech, dry understated humor he never performs, grumbling complaints that are really affection, and a quiet cautious undertone. Sounds like a man who trusts stone and weight more than theories. Chest resonance, relaxed pacing. Studio-quality recording, clean, no background noise.

**Preview:** I believe in not insulting whatever sits under this much earth. I'm tired of walking into the same hole twice. I can hate things nearby.

## Enhedu

**Prompt:** A woman in her early 30s with a crisp, clear, low-mid voice and a neutral accent. Quick-witted, sharp and observant, with a dry amused edge she keeps understated. Confident, street-smart and alert, always weighing the price of things. Never bubbly. Natural conversational pacing. Studio-quality recording, clean, no background noise.

**Preview:** You two bicker like a married pair. I said no to him. I didn't say no to you. Everything has a price. Some prices are better left unpaid.

## Ninsun (Ninsun Etana)

**Prompt:** A woman in her late 20s with a clear, precise, mid-pitched voice and a neutral accent. A scholar who speaks formally and carefully, often without contractions, with dry academic humor and quiet intensity. Level and controlled, becoming sharper and more exact rather than louder when frightened. Studio-quality recording, clean, no background noise.

**Preview:** People talk exactly enough. I'm not asking to be hired. I'm telling you I already know more about the symbol on your skin than the Collector does, and I am coming with you whether you like the arrangement or not.

## Kurash (Chapter 1 only, plus memories)

**Prompt:** A young man around 19 with a light, bright, eager voice and a neutral accent. Curious and excitable, talks a little fast, laughs a little too quickly, hungry for a better life. Earnest rather than foolish, with a hint of restless impatience. Studio-quality recording, clean, no background noise.

**Preview:** My uncle always said the old kings hid their treasure because they feared thieves. So why bury it at all? That's impossible. It's unfair.

## Bel-iddin (Zik's father)

**Prompt:** An older man in his 60s with a low, warm, slightly weathered voice and a neutral accent. A patient, precise teacher, grave and intimate, speaking with calm certainty and slow deliberate pacing, as if sharing hard-won wisdom with his son. Studio-quality recording, clean, no background noise.

**Preview:** Read the mortar, not the bricks. Bricks lie. Men shape them to look holy or expensive. Nobody bothers faking the mortar.

## The Collector

**Prompt:** A refined, narrow, middle-aged man in his late 50s with a smooth, quiet, dry voice and a neutral accent. Patient, unhurried and faintly amused, a dealer in antiquities who never raises his voice and always seems to know more than he says. Polished and courteous with a cold undertone. Close and intimate. Studio-quality recording, clean, no background noise.

**Preview:** The desert is famously responsible for the poor decisions of men. A man who claims to know everything is selling religion. I am selling time.

## The Dead King (crowned skeleton)

**Prompt:** An ancient, dead king speaking through damaged, dried-out vocal cords. A brittle, scratchy, nearly whispered voice, slow and unnervingly calm, quiet but absolutely certain and threatening. Each word forced out with effort and every consonant still clear. No growl, no monster roar. Studio-quality recording, clean, no background noise.

**Preview:** Dust settles. Stone forgets. But I remember every name that was ever spoken in this room.

## Mardu (lower-archive custodian, Ch. 16)

**Prompt:** An old archivist in his late 60s with a dry, steady, slightly papery voice and a neutral accent. Not frail, worn smooth by decades of repetition. Bone-dry bureaucratic wit delivered with a completely straight face, patient and unhurried, quietly kind underneath. Studio-quality recording, clean, no background noise.

**Preview:** I have spent thirty-one years listening to people become quiet on the other side of it. Water dislikes bureaucracy. The builders gave it a private route.

## Zik's mother (Ch. 5)

**Prompt:** A frail woman in her early 60s with a soft, low, slightly hoarse voice and a neutral accent. Ill and tired but sharp-eyed and unsentimental, speaking quietly and plainly, seeing straight through her son. Occasional weariness in the breath. Studio-quality recording, clean, no background noise.

**Preview:** You look like your father when he came back from that last trip. Same eyes. Same lie. I think your father believed that knowing gave him the right to touch it.

## The old scribe instructor (Ch. 5)

**Prompt:** An old scribe teacher in his 70s with a gravelly, dry, gently sardonic voice and a neutral accent. Slow, wry and a little cantankerous, a lifelong teacher who has corrected ten thousand mistakes. Studio-quality recording, clean, no background noise.

**Preview:** He'd argue with anyone willing to waste an afternoon. If you discover the answer, write it down before someone important changes it. Again.

## The false temple scribe (Ch. 18)

**Prompt:** A young man in his early 20s with a clean, educated, slightly nervous voice and a neutral accent. A well-dressed temple scribe, polite and anxious, swallowing his words under pressure. Studio-quality recording, clean, no background noise.

**Preview:** The Collector sends his regards. He would like to speak with you again. The Collector is prepared to offer ten times what you received today.

## The Man Who Refused (Ch. 27–28)

**Prompt:** A gaunt, exhausted man of indeterminate age with a thin, hoarse, unused-sounding voice and a neutral accent. Speaks slowly and quietly, as if his voice has not been used in years, with weary calm and flashes of bleak dry humor. Studio-quality recording, clean, no background noise.

**Preview:** You're real. I thought the City had finally learned how to make better copies. You are being returned. Circles would be kinder.

## The Statue's voice (end of Ch. 30)

**Prompt:** A dry, flat, utterly unimpressed voice, neither young nor old, with a neutral accent. Bored and bureaucratic, like a clerk checking a name off an endless list. Speaks slowly with no emotion at all, coming from somewhere hollow and deep. Studio-quality recording, clean, no background noise.

**Preview:** You are late. The account was opened long ago. Every name is recorded, and every debt is eventually collected.

## Minor roles (or use library voices and save credits)

### Apprentice Boy (Ch.5)

**Prompt:** A shy boy around 12 with a light, young, high voice and a neutral accent. Respectful and a little nervous, speaking softly to an adult he admires. Studio-quality recording, clean, no background noise.

**Preview (test only):** Master Zikir. No, Master. But my instructor does. He says your father used to stand right there every market day.

### Messenger Boy (Ch.11)

**Prompt:** A street-smart boy around 14 with a cracking adolescent voice and a neutral accent. Cagey, impatient with questions, quick and a little sullen, eager for a coin. Studio-quality recording, clean, no background noise.

**Preview (test only):** Man said to give this to the ones cutting marks on the walls. Said you'd know. He had clean hands. Everyone around here has dirty hands.

### Water-Seller (Ch.9)

**Prompt:** A loud, indignant street vendor in his 40s with a nasal, quick, working-class voice and a neutral accent. Comic, defensive, always arguing about measures. Studio-quality recording, clean, no background noise.

**Preview (test only):** I filled the cup to the line. The cup is old. I measured it in front of you. My thumb is not that large.

### Water Customer (Ch.9)

**Prompt:** A sour, deadpan customer in his 50s with a dry, low, unimpressed voice and a neutral accent. Flat comic delivery, accusing without raising his voice. Studio-quality recording, clean, no background noise.

**Preview (test only):** The line is crooked. So is your cheating. With your thumb in the cup. It has always been that large.

### Archive Guard (Ch.16)

**Prompt:** A bored temple guard in his 30s with a gruff, heavy, impatient voice and a neutral accent, calling down through a door. Suspicion slowly replacing laziness. Studio-quality recording, clean, no background noise.

**Preview (test only):** Mardu? Mardu, are you down there? You have been down there too long. This hinge looks fine. Mardu, open this.

### Inn Cook (Ch.19)

**Prompt:** A harried inn cook in her 40s with a loud, sharp, no-nonsense voice and a neutral accent, waving a ladle at intruders in her kitchen. Studio-quality recording, clean, no background noise.

**Preview (test only):** Out. Out of my kitchen, all of you, before I put the ladle somewhere you will not enjoy. What was that? What was that noise?

### The City (non-human voice)

**Prompt:** An inhuman, wet, whispering voice that seems to come from deep stone and water at once, with a neutral accent. Slow, patient, intimate and wrong, every word breathed rather than spoken. Studio-quality recording, clean, no background noise.

**Preview (test only):** Ammaru. Ammaru. A name spoken is a door left open, and every door I have ever been given, I have kept.

### The Remainder (Ch.38)

**Do not design a new voice.** Use Zik's voice. The script tags his lines `[exhausted]`. The book says he "sounded exactly like him."

### City mimicry

Lines tagged `[eerie]` are the City imitating someone (Bel-iddin, Ninsun, Zik). They're assigned to that character's own voice on purpose.
