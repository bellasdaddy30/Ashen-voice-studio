# The First City — Casting Bible (full book)

**Source:** `The_First_City_Publication_Ready.pages`. All 40 chapters, the Book Two preview and the
author's note were read in full (about 75,700 words). Character cards in
`book-data/characters/character-cards.json` still control speed, pause and pitch. This file shapes
the *base voices* and the look of each character.

**Legend**
- **Text** = stated in the manuscript (quoted where useful).
- **Interpretation** = my reading. Overrule it freely.
- **Marketing-safe** = whether the look/voice notes can be used publicly without spoiling the book.

## 0. Ground rules for the whole production

### Accent
Nobody knows how Akkadian or Sumerian speech would sound in English, and the production guide already
says *avoid exaggerated accents unless established as canon*. So:
- Use one consistent, neutral English for the whole cast. No "movie Arab," no Persian caricature, no
  Shakespearean "ancient" voice.
- Let "ancient" come from **pacing, restraint and texture**: dry, sun-worn, low. Not from an accent.
- If you want any colour at all, apply a *very faint* Middle Eastern warmth to **every** character
  equally. Applying it to just one character reads as a stereotype.

### Speech patterns already in the prose (use them in casting)
These come straight from the text. They're the most useful thing to direct each voice with.

| Character | How they talk on the page |
|---|---|
| Zik | Short. Dry. Deflects with logic ("That's not an answer." / "It is."). Says "Nothing" when it's something. |
| Balag | Plain words, complaint as affection, profanity under stress ("Same fucking math"). |
| Enhedu | Uses contractions and swears; everything framed as price, value or risk. After Ch. 29 her affect goes **flat**. |
| Ninsun | **Formal. Few contractions.** "That is not the same thing." "It is called a test." Precise, and pedantic when scared. |
| Collector | Polished, patient, answers a different question than the one asked. Never raises his voice. |
| Mardu | Bone-dry bureaucratic wit ("I am old. Everything takes too long."). |

### The look of the world (shared)
**Interpretation**, grounded in Mesopotamian archaeology and in the text:
- **People:** olive to deep-brown skin, dark hair, dark eyes. Faces weathered by sun and wind. Men
  mostly bearded.
- **Clothing:** undyed or earth-tone wool and linen; wrapped tunics and robes; fringed shawls
  (**Text**: Enhedu's shawl fringe); wool cloaks; leather sandals and boots; head cloths against sand.
  Dust and mud in everything.
- **Gear (Text):** bronze knives and awls, styluses, clay tablets, waxed cord, chalk, charcoal,
  torches and oil lamps, spears, an axe, a hammer, rope, donkeys.
- **Uruk-Under-the-Sun (Text):** smoke, onions, spiced lamb, dye-works dripping red and indigo,
  tanneries, wine shops, the Street of Scribes, kilns, temple platforms that "burned red" at dawn.
- **The First City (Text):** oily black stone that swallows torchlight; towers at impossible angles;
  bridges that cross themselves; names gouged from lintels; white limestone walls of names; breathing
  corridors; a library of glass jars.

### Name notes (real-world roots)
- *Bel-iddin*: a real Akkadian name pattern, "Bel has given."
- *Ashur*: the god and city of Assyria.
- *Enhedu*: echoes *Enheduanna*, high priestess and the earliest named author in history.
- *Balag*: a Sumerian instrument played in temple laments.
- *Kurash*: the ancient form of the name we know as Cyrus.
- *Ninsun*: in myth, the goddess-mother of Gilgamesh. *Etana* (her family name, **Text**): a
  legendary early king of Kish.
- *Naram* (Bel-iddin's father, **Text**): echoes Naram-Sin of Akkad.

## 1. MAIN CAST

### Narrator
**Text / card:** restrained, dry, observant; lets unease come from the prose.
**Range needed across the book:** sunlit banter (Ch. 1–4), city noir (Ch. 5–21), desert dread
(Ch. 22–25), cosmic horror and grief (Ch. 26–40). Chapter 40 is told from **Ninsun's point of view**,
so the narrator should soften slightly there.

> **Prompt:** An audiobook narrator, a man in his late 40s with a low, warm, slightly dry and sun-worn voice and a neutral accent. Intelligent, restrained and observant, never theatrical. Measured pacing around 150 words per minute, close and intimate, letting dread build quietly through understatement. Suited to dark archaeological fantasy. Studio-quality recording, clean, no background noise.
>
> **Preview:** The desert spat secrets when it felt like it. Today it gave up a ridge of brick half-buried in sand. A windstorm had stripped the dunes down to hard earth and left the upper edge exposed.

Marketing-safe: **yes.**

### Zik (Zikir Ashur)
**Text:**
- Lead archaeologist.
- Taught by his father to read mortar.
- Was twelve at the first lesson.
- Has "spent twelve years looking for his father."
- Supports a sick mother with "silver to keep his mother in medicine."
- Carries a bronze awl and knife, and repairs things that aren't his (straps, walls).
- A spiral-drop mark is burned into his left palm, kept wrapped in linen.
- Late in the book, gray "necrosis" climbs his left arm, and the arm finally ceases to exist.

**Age (Interpretation):** **early to mid 30s.** The twelve-year search, the twenty-years-ago tablet
and a living mother all point younger than I guessed before.

**Look (Interpretation):** lean and wiry, not big. Short dark beard with grit in it. Squint lines
from reading mortar in hard sun. Callused, cord-nicked fingers. Linen-wrapped left hand. A plain,
dust-coloured tunic and cloak, nothing that signals money. He looks at walls before people.

**Voice:** mid-low, dry, flat delivery of jokes, logic as armour.
- **Arc:** steady through Ch. 1–21.
- Thinner and more guarded from Ch. 24, after he starts losing memories.
- Physically weakening in Ch. 39–40, with breath breaking through his teeth.

> **Prompt:** A man in his early 30s with a mid-low, dry, quietly confident voice and a neutral accent. Skeptical, precise and economical with words, with deadpan humor delivered completely flat. Calm and controlled, explaining technical things as if they are obvious, with a guarded weariness underneath. Natural conversational pacing. Studio-quality recording, clean, no background noise.
>
> **Preview:** Clay melts if you get it hot enough. Not campfire hot. Kiln hot. Hot enough that whoever built this either knew something we've forgotten, or they were trying to seal something badly enough that they invented a way.

Marketing-safe: base look **yes**. The gray arm and missing arm are **no** (spoilers).

### Balag
**Text:**
- "The big man," "wide enough to block a good portion of the desert," with a beard full of sand.
- Carries a spear, later also an axe, and at one point a hammer. Wears a wool cloak.
- Touches two fingers to his chest in "the old ward his grandfather had taught him," and repeats
  his grandfather's stories of seven gates.
- Gentle with donkeys. Checks every strap twice.
- Lost a 15-year-old boy on his first crew.
- Profane under stress.

**Look (Interpretation):** late 40s to early 50s. Huge through the shoulders and chest. Thick full
beard and heavy brow. Broad, scarred hands. Spear on his shoulder and axe at his back. Wool cloak. A
small amulet or cord at his chest that he touches for the ward.

**Voice:** deep, slow, grounded. Complaints delivered like weather reports. The fear underneath shows
only in the Ch. 31–32 memory loss ("Who?").

> **Prompt:** A very large man in his late 40s with a deep, heavy, grounded voice and a neutral accent. Slow, economical speech, dry understated humor he never performs, grumbling complaints that are really affection, and a quiet cautious undertone. Sounds like a man who trusts stone and weight more than theories. Chest resonance, relaxed pacing. Studio-quality recording, clean, no background noise.
>
> **Preview:** I believe in not insulting whatever sits under this much earth. I'm tired of walking into the same hole twice. I can hate things nearby.

(The middle sentence is softened from "same fucking hole" so the preview stays clean. Use the real
line in production.)

Marketing-safe: **yes.**

**Text:**
- "Smaller than Balag… but people moved out of her way more quickly."
- Knife tucked under her arm. Lockpicks and hooks.
- A fringed shawl. Pale hair wrapping, later swapped for dark cloth for night travel.
- Turns a "small copper figurine" with a smiling face worn smooth.

Record her post-vision lines as a separate pass with an emotion setting turned down. Don't swap

> **Prompt:** A woman in her early 30s with a crisp, clear, low-mid voice and a neutral accent. Quick-witted, sharp and observant, with a dry amused edge she keeps understated. Confident, street-smart and alert, always weighing the price of things. Never bubbly. Natural conversational pacing. Studio-quality recording, clean, no background noise.
>
> **Preview:** You two bicker like a married pair. I said no to him. I didn't say no to you. Everything has a price. Some prices are better left unpaid.

### Ninsun (Ninsun Etana)
**Text:**
- Scholar who left the university ("Not anymore"). Now works the lower archives.
- "Ink stained both wrists." Fingers stained with ink and stone dust.
- "Shorter than Enhedu… learned to take up less space than she needed."
- Her sister vanished into a shifted district three years ago. The sister shares her "dark eyes" and
  "narrow jaw."
- Carries a chipped bronze stylus her sister gave her (six years).
- Voice "stayed level, but something underneath it had gone hard."
- She survives the book and carries Balag's axe head and Enhedu's figurine out into the desert.

**Look (Interpretation):** late 20s. Slight, narrow-jawed, dark-eyed. Hair pinned back, with strands
escaping. A stylus behind her ear. A satchel of tablets clutched to her chest. Ink to the wrists. Dark
circles deepen as the book goes on. Ch. 38: a blood-darkened sleeve.

**Voice:** precise, mid-pitched, formal (few contractions), with dry academic humour. Under strain she
gets *more* exact, not louder. This is the main contrast with Enhedu: Enhedu is street, Ninsun is
archive.

> **Prompt:** A woman in her late 20s with a clear, precise, mid-pitched voice and a neutral accent. A scholar who speaks formally and carefully, often without contractions, with dry academic humor and quiet intensity. Level and controlled, becoming sharper and more exact rather than louder when frightened. Studio-quality recording, clean, no background noise.
>
> **Preview:** People talk exactly enough. I'm not asking to be hired. I'm telling you I already know more about the symbol on your skin than the Collector does, and I am coming with you whether you like the arrangement or not.

Marketing-safe: **yes** (just don't reveal that she's the one who walks out).

### Bel-iddin (Zik's father)
**Text:**
- "Bel-iddin, son of Naram, surveyor by training, liar by necessity."
- Broke the bone under his smallest finger, so his handwriting hooks.
- Coiled his measuring cord clockwise. Wore a cloak smelling of "smoke and myrrh."
- Carried the brass compass with "the dent along the rim, the tiny scratch across the glass."
- In Zik's vision (Ch. 24): "hair streaked with gray, face thinner, clothing torn and stained."
- In the jar (Ch. 39): "gray hair. A beard worn shorter… slight bend in his shoulders," tapping
  sand with a walking stick.
- The old scribe remembers him arguing history every market day.

**Look (Interpretation):** 60s. Lean like Zik. Gray-streaked short beard. Deeply lined. A walking
stick. Compass at his belt. Patient hands, crooked smallest finger.

**Voice:** older, warm, low, deliberate.
- **Memory lines:** calm certainty.
- **Journal lines in his hand:** grave, getting more urgent as the book goes on.
- **Ch. 24 vision line** ("You shouldn't be able to see this."): sorrow, not fear.

> **Prompt:** An older man in his 60s with a low, warm, slightly weathered voice and a neutral accent. A patient, precise teacher, grave and intimate, speaking with calm certainty and slow deliberate pacing, as if sharing hard-won wisdom with his son. Studio-quality recording, clean, no background noise.
>
> **Preview:** Read the mortar, not the bricks. Bricks lie. Men shape them to look holy or expensive. Nobody bothers faking the mortar.

Marketing-safe: **yes.**

### The Collector
**Text:**
- "A narrow man with a narrow face, a narrow beard, and the sort of eyes that made people check
  whether their purses were still attached."
- His name is deliberately forgotten.
- Wears a robe. "Long fingers. Bare. No rings."
- Looks "almost regretful."
- His voice sounds closer than he is ("You are standing farther away than you sound." / "Practice.").
- Knew Bel-iddin twenty years ago.
- **Spoiler detail:** leaves wet handprints climbing a wall, fingers pointing *downward*.

**Look (Interpretation):** 50s to 60s, ageless in the way that bothers people. Tall and thin, with a
neat narrow beard. Fine but plain dark robe. Long, very clean pale hands. Stillness. Never seen
hurrying.

**Voice:** smooth, quiet, dry, faintly amused, patient. He never raises it.
- **Production idea:** give him a slightly *close-miked, intimate* sound even when he's "outside the
  shutter." That matches the text's trick of sounding nearer than he is.

> **Prompt:** A refined, narrow, middle-aged man in his late 50s with a smooth, quiet, dry voice and a neutral accent. Patient, unhurried and faintly amused, a dealer in antiquities who never raises his voice and always seems to know more than he says. Polished and courteous with a cold undertone. Close and intimate. Studio-quality recording, clean, no background noise.
>
> **Preview:** The desert is famously responsible for the poor decisions of men. A man who claims to know everything is selling religion. I am selling time.

Marketing-safe: voice and base look **yes**. The handprints and what he might be: **no**.

## 3. THE CITY'S VOICES (production approach, not new actors)

The text is explicit that the City **imitates**:
- The basin says "Ammaru" in its own voice, then *"in Ninsun's voice"* (Ch. 9).
- The corridor repeats Balag "stretched thin and wet," then speaks "in Zik's voice" (Ch. 30).
- In the desert, Bel-iddin's voice comes "without his father's rhythm. Every pause was wrong. Every
  breath arrived half a heartbeat too late" (Ch. 23).

So:
- **Mimic lines** = the original character's voice plus processing: low-pass, a slight pitch drift,
  a short wet reverb, and **timing edits** (stretch the pauses, shift the breaths late).
- **The basin's own first "Ammaru"** = a whispered, wet, not-quite-human take. Chatterbox from a
  whispered reference works.
- **Journal lines** (the "self-writing ink"):
  - Lines in **Bel-iddin's hand** → Bel-iddin's voice (matches your existing production guide).
  - Lines in **"a hand that was neither his father's nor his own"** (e.g. NOT YET, THE CITY IS TIRED
    OF WAITING, ONE OF YOU WAS NEVER SENT) → either the Narrator in a colder, slower read, or the
    Statue voice heavily whispered. My recommendation: **Narrator, colder**. It keeps the mystery
    and costs no extra voice.
- **Carved and clay messages** (e.g. Ammaru's "I kept it closed.") are read aloud by Ninsun in the
  text, so they stay in Ninsun's voice.

## 6. Things that affect the audiobook build (please check)

1. **Chapter 1 text mismatch.** The repo's `manuscript.txt` has about 340 words that are **not** in
   `The_First_City_Publication_Ready.pages`. They include several sensory beats ("Dust rasped between
   the fragment and the pad of his thumb", "The laugh struck the stone and came back larger") and the
   entire "Zik held everyone at the threshold…" salt-and-settled-floor passage. Decide which version
   is canon before rendering.
2. **Attribution of "…is still waiting below."** Ch. 17 ends with **Zik** finishing Enhedu's
   sentence. Ch. 18 opens "No one spoke after **Bel-iddin's** final words. …is still waiting below."
   The parser needs one answer. Either the Ch. 18 opener is meant as "Bel-iddin's final words [in the
   journal]," or it's a slip.
3. **Kurash and the black floor (Ch. 19).** RESOLVED: intentional per the author. Both versions are true; the narrator plays the memory straight.
4. **Enhedu's two figurines.** The copper one with the smiling face (Ch. 2, carried out by Ninsun) and
   the golden one from her vision (Ch. 29) are different objects. Fine as written, but worth knowing so
   art doesn't merge them.
