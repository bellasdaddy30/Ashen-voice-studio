Legend:
- **Text** = stated in the manuscript.
- **Interpretation** = my reading, grounded in the text and Mesopotamian history. Overrule freely.

---

## Ground rules for the whole cast

**Accent.** Nobody knows what Akkadian or Sumerian speech would sound like rendered in English, and
the production guide already says *avoid exaggerated accents unless later established as canon*. So:
- One consistent, neutral English for everyone. No "movie Arab," no Persian caricature, no
  Shakespearean "ancient" voice.
- "Ancientness" comes from pacing, restraint and texture (dry, sun-worn, low), not from an accent.
- If you want any colour at all, the safest is a *very faint* Middle Eastern warmth applied to every
  character equally — never to one person, or it reads as a stereotype.

**Look (shared world).** **Interpretation**, based on Mesopotamian art and archaeology:
- Olive to deep-brown skin, dark hair, dark eyes, faces weathered by sun and wind.
- Men usually bearded. Hair and beards practical on a dig, not the oiled ringlets of palace reliefs.
- Clothing: undyed or earth-toned wool and linen; wrapped kilts and tunics; fringed shawls; leather
  sandals; head cloths against sand. Sand in everything (**Text**: "Sand had worked into his beard
  and the folds of his clothes").
- Tools: bronze knives, an awl, iron bars and tools, spears, torches, rope (**Text**).
- Kohl around the eyes was used in the ancient Near East and doubles as sun glare protection — a
  nice visual touch for desert crew. (Interpretation.)

**Name notes** (real-world roots, worth knowing even if they're not plot points):
- *Bel-iddin* — a genuine Akkadian name pattern, "Bel has given."
- *Ashur* — the god and city of Assyria.
- *Enhedu* — echoes *Enheduanna*, the Akkadian high priestess and earliest named author in history.
- *Balag* — a Sumerian instrument used in temple laments. Fitting for a big, solemn, warding man.
- *Kurash* — the Elamite/Akkadian rendering of the name we know as Cyrus.

## Narrator

**Text / card:** adult male; intelligent, restrained, dry, observant; "lets the unease come from the
prose rather than announcing it." Measured at the dig, cooler during the descent, spacious in the
tomb, controlled shock after Kurash dies.

**Sound:** a voice that has stood in a lot of ruins. Low, unhurried, a little dry, close to the mic.
Never a trailer-voice.

ElevenLabs Voice Design prompt:
> An audiobook narrator, a man in his late 40s with a low, warm, slightly dry and sun-worn voice and a neutral accent. Intelligent, restrained and observant, never theatrical. Measured pacing around 150 words per minute, close and intimate, letting dread build quietly through understatement. Suited to dark archaeological fantasy. Studio-quality recording, clean, no background noise.

Preview text (from the manuscript):
> The desert spat secrets when it felt like it. Today it gave up a ridge of brick half-buried in sand. A windstorm had stripped the dunes down to hard earth and left the upper edge exposed.

## Zik — Zikir Ashur (protagonist)

**Text / card:** lead archaeologist; dry, skeptical, technically precise; "rarely wastes words";
emotion held under control; learned from his father since at least age twelve; once had Kurash's
hunger ("He had worn that same look once"). Breaks control only around his father's compass and journal.

**Look (Interpretation):** late 30s to mid 40s. Lean, wiry, not big — Balag is the wall, Zik is the
eye. Close-cropped dark beard with grit in it, crow's feet from squinting at mortar in hard sun,
scarred and callused fingertips. Bronze knife at the belt. Plain dust-coloured tunic and head cloth,
nothing that signals wealth. A habit of looking at walls instead of people.

**Sound:** mid-low, dry, quiet confidence. Jokes delivered flat. Technical lines easy, never lecturing.

ElevenLabs Voice Design prompt:
> A man around 40 with a mid-low, dry, quietly confident voice and a neutral accent. Skeptical, precise and economical with words, with deadpan humor delivered completely flat. Calm and controlled, explaining technical things effortlessly as if they are obvious. A slight weariness under the surface. Natural conversational pacing. Studio-quality recording, clean, no background noise.

Preview text (from the manuscript):
> Clay melts if you get it hot enough. Not campfire hot. Kiln hot. Hot enough that whoever built this either knew something we've forgotten, or they were trying to seal something badly enough that they invented a way.

## Balag (veteran crewman / protector)

**Text / card:** "The big man," spear on one shoulder, "wide enough to block a good portion of the
desert," sand in his beard, "particular stillness," leads the descent because he's largest, touches
two fingers to his chest in his grandfather's old ward. Dry humour he never performs.

**Look (Interpretation):** 40s to 50s. Huge through the shoulders and chest, thick full beard, heavy
brow, broad hands. Spear always within reach. Simple wrapped kilt and shawl, maybe a cord or small
amulet at the chest he touches when he makes the ward.

**Sound:** deep, slow, grounded. Each line sounds weighed before it's spent.

ElevenLabs Voice Design prompt:
> A very large man in his late 40s with a deep, heavy, grounded voice and a neutral accent. Slow, economical speech, dry understated humor he never performs, and a quiet cautious undertone. Sounds like a man who trusts stone and weight more than theories. Low pitch, chest resonance, relaxed pacing. Studio-quality recording, clean, no background noise.

Preview text (from the manuscript):
> I believe in not insulting whatever sits under this much earth. Keep talking. I'll convert you. The old kings were the thieves.

## Enhedu (crew member / sharp observer)

**Text / card:** turns a broken seal "the way other people worried a coin, testing for value"; knife
tucked under one arm; teases the men; studies "the seams rather than the wealth"; knife "in her hand
before the sound finished." Smile "slow and sharp."

**Look (Interpretation):** late 20s to early 30s. Compact, quick, alert. Dark hair bound back and
wrapped against sand, kohl-lined eyes, a thin scar or two on her hands from handling broken pottery
and seals. Knife always tucked under her arm or in her hand. Moves and looks like the first person to
notice the exit.

**Sound:** crisp, mid-pitch, quick. Amused without being flippant; narrows into focus as they descend.

ElevenLabs Voice Design prompt:
> A woman around 30 with a crisp, clear, mid-pitched voice and a neutral accent. Quick-witted, sharp and observant, with a dry amused edge she keeps understated. Confident and alert, never bubbly. Teasing lines are low-key; serious lines are focused and steady. Natural conversational pacing. Studio-quality recording, clean, no background noise.

Preview text (from the manuscript):
> You two bicker like a married pair. I said no to him. I didn't say no to you. Whatever's inside wasn't trying to leave.

## Kurash (young new crewman)

**Text / card:** three weeks with the group; laughs "too loud and too fast"; called "the boy";
copies Zik's motions "with exaggerated care"; dreams of "a house. A horse. A woman."; eager, curious,
not stupid.

**Look (Interpretation):** 18 to early 20s. Thin, sun-browned, barely a beard. Cheap, slightly
oversized clothing, maybe borrowed. Eager eyes that keep drifting toward anything that shines.

**Sound:** lighter and brighter than the rest, a touch faster. Wanting, not villainous.

ElevenLabs Voice Design prompt:
> A young man around 19 with a light, bright, eager voice and a neutral accent. Curious and excitable, talks a little fast, laughs a little too quickly, hungry for a better life. Earnest rather than foolish, with a hint of restless impatience. Studio-quality recording, clean, no background noise.

Preview text (from the manuscript):
> My uncle always said the old kings hid their treasure because they feared thieves. So why bury it at all? That's impossible. It's unfair.

## Bel-iddin (Zik's father — memory and journal voice)

**Text / card:** older, precise, intimate, grave; a teacher who expects Zik to think; scraped mortar
"with the tip of a bronze knife"; carried the brass compass with "the worn leather case, the dent along
the rim, the tiny scratch across the glass"; disappeared. The final journal line is the same man under strain.

**Look (Interpretation):** 60s in memory. Grey-streaked beard, deeply lined face, the same lean frame
Zik has. Patient hands. The compass on a cord or at his belt.

**Sound:** older, warm, low, deliberate. Calm certainty in memory; pressed urgency in the last journal line.

ElevenLabs Voice Design prompt:
> An older man in his 60s with a low, warm, slightly weathered voice and a neutral accent. A patient, precise teacher, grave and intimate, speaking with calm certainty and slow deliberate pacing, as if sharing hard-won wisdom with his son. Studio-quality recording, clean, no background noise.

Preview text (from the manuscript):
> Read the mortar, not the bricks. Bricks lie. Men shape them to look holy or expensive. Nobody bothers faking the mortar.

## Dead King (crowned skeleton)

**Text / card:** skeleton on a stone throne, "last scraps of royal robes," "a crown of blackened
silver" bearing the spiral and drop. One line: "Zikir Ashur." The card says the voice is
reference-conditioned by Chatterbox from a real gravelly whispered performance — **keep that approach**.
It's already the right call.

**Look:** for imagery and marketing, keep it to what's on the page (throne, bones, blackened crown,
the symbol), and keep the mystery.

ElevenLabs Voice Design prompt, if you ever want an ElevenLabs version:
> An ancient, dead king speaking through damaged, dried-out vocal cords. A brittle, scratchy, nearly whispered voice, slow and unnervingly calm, quiet but absolutely certain and threatening. Each word forced out with effort and every consonant still clear. No growl, no monster roar. Studio-quality recording, clean, no background noise.

Preview text (**test-only, NOT canon** — Voice Design needs a longer line than his single one):
> Dust settles. Stone forgets. But I remember every name that was ever spoken in this room.

## Using these

1. Generate the **Narrator first** and lock it. Design everyone else to contrast with it in pitch or
   texture; listeners separate speakers by contrast.
2. Generate all characters with previews at a similar emotional level, so they sit together in a scene.
3. Character cards in `book-data/characters/character-cards.json` remain the source of truth for
   speed, pause and pitch. These prompts only shape the base voice.
