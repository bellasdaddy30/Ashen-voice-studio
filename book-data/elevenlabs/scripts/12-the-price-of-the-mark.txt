Narrator: The first real cost arrived two days after the warning note.
Narrator: They had been working a narrow cut behind the tanners' row—two notches of their own, no forged third, nothing that should have drawn attention. The passage was short and already starting to close when BAH-lahg's spear found something soft under the dust at the far end.
Narrator: He stopped. Everyone stopped.
Narrator: It was a pack. Ordinary. Canvas and leather, the kind any lower-district porter carried. The straps had been cut clean through. Inside was a water skin, a half-eaten loaf gone hard, and a small clay tablet wrapped in cloth. No name. No identifying marks. Just the ordinary gear of someone who had expected to walk out again.
Narrator: NEEN-soon unwrapped the tablet. The writing was hurried and already fading at the edges, as if the clay itself had decided the words were temporary.
Narrator: Three lines:
Narrator: [slowly] THEY ARE CUTTING THE WALLS
Narrator: [slowly] THE CITY DOES NOT LIKE WITNESSES
Narrator: [slowly] I SHOULD NOT HAVE FOLLOWED THE MARKS
