Narrator: From inside BAH-lahg's pack the fourth knock still hung in the air, unanswered. No one moved to open the straps. BAH-lahg had gone still in the particular way he did when the ground itself had become untrustworthy. En-KHEH-doo watched the canvas without reaching for it, the figurine turning once in her fingers and then forgotten. NEEN-soon stood two feet from them, hands clenched white at her sides, and neither of the others looked at her.
Narrator: They were not looking through her.
Narrator: They were looking past her entirely, the way a person's eyes slide off a shadow that has not quite decided whether it wants to be noticed.

Narrator: BAH-lahg said. He stood and slung the pack over one shoulder. The leather straps groaned under the weight of whatever still moved inside it.
Balag: [impatient] Zeek? You coming, or are you going to keep staring at your book?
Narrator: Zeek looked from BAH-lahg to NEEN-soon.
Narrator: She was visibly terrified and just as visibly refusing to stop existing because the world around her had decided she was an inconvenience to be filed away.
Ninsun: [whispers] Zeek,
Narrator: she whispered. Her voice hitched on his name.
Ninsun: [whispers] They don't hear me. It's like I'm not even making sound.
Narrator: He took a slow breath. His hand drifted to the journal, then past it. He had told himself he would not bargain with the City again. He knew exactly what it cost—the holes it punched cleanly through history, the strange smooth empty spaces that remained afterward like scars with nothing underneath them. The worst part had never been the clean forgetting. It was knowing that something important had been taken and having no shape left behind to mourn.
Narrator: Looking at NEEN-soon changed the arithmetic.
Narrator: The shadows in the room seemed to curl toward her. The stone behind her had begun to soften at the edges, the way limestone softened when the ledger was preparing to close an account.
Narrator: He spoke without raising his voice.
Zik: [firmly] Her name is NEEN-soon. She is with us. She stays.
Narrator: For a moment nothing answered.
Narrator: Then BAH-lahg blinked, once, hard, as if something behind his eyes had been forcibly rethreaded. He looked at NEEN-soon with the startled recognition of a man who has just remembered a debt he did not know he owed.
Balag: [confused] NEEN-soon,
Narrator: he said. The name arrived incomplete, as though retrieved from a damaged tablet.
Balag: [confused] Right. The scholar.
Narrator: En-KHEH-doo's gaze slid toward her and held. Not with warmth. With the remote attention she now gave anything that had been declared relevant by someone else.
Narrator: NEEN-soon exhaled a sound that was almost a sob and almost nothing.
Narrator: Zeek felt the cost arrive a heartbeat later.
Narrator: It was not dramatic. A single clean absence opened somewhere behind his ribs—the specific reason he had decided she was worth the price. He still knew her name. He still knew she belonged with them. He still knew he had paid something irreversible to keep her present in the others' minds.
Narrator: He simply could no longer remember what had made her worth that price in the first place.
Narrator: BAH-lahg shifted the pack on his shoulder. Whatever was inside it had gone quiet.
Balag: [confused] What did you just do, Zeek? Why is she still here?
Narrator: Zeek looked at her, then at the wall of names behind them.
Zik: [quietly] Because I decided she was.
Narrator: He was not entirely sure, even as he said it, whether he believed his own answer.
Narrator: They began walking. NEEN-soon stayed close enough behind him that he could hear the rhythm of her breathing. He knew her name. He knew she belonged with them.
Narrator: The rest was gone.
