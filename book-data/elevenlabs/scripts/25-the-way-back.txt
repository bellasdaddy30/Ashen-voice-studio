CHAPTER 25 — THE WAY BACK

Narrator: CHAPTER 25
[pause 1s]
Narrator: The Way Back
[pause 2s]
Narrator: Zeek woke before dawn to silence.
Narrator: Real silence—the kind the desert had not offered since they found the staircase. No wind moved across the dunes. No night birds called. Even the donkeys stood still, ears down, as if holding their breath with him.
Narrator: He lay beneath his blanket and listened for the knocking. Nothing came.
Narrator: Slowly he reached for the journal. The cover was colder than the desert night had any right to leave it. He opened it anyway. No new lines. The pages sat exactly as he had left them. The mark on his palm had gone cold too. For the first time since the tomb it looked like nothing more than an old scar catching the wrong light.
Narrator: For one treacherous second he let himself believe it was over.
Narrator: Then he checked his father's compass. The needle no longer pointed into the earth. It pointed back toward OO-rook—steady, almost eager.
Narrator: He did not trust it. He got up anyway.
Narrator: He woke BAH-lahg first. The big man cracked one eye open.
Balag: [groggy] What?
Zik: [quietly] We're leaving.
Narrator: BAH-lahg did not argue. He looked toward the staircase, said nothing, and started rolling his blanket. That silence frightened Zeek more than any argument would have.
Narrator: NEEN-soon agreed before he finished the sentence. She had slept badly; the hollows under her eyes showed it. En-KHEH-doo said nothing at all. Her silence had teeth.
[pause 1.5s]
Narrator: They broke camp in the dark and put their backs to the ridge.
Narrator: For the first few hours the desert played along. The sky went from black to bruised purple to the flat white of morning. Heat crawled up out of the sand in waves you could watch. Their shadows stretched long and blue, then shrank beneath them as the sun climbed. Zeek navigated by the old caravan markers and his father's map, the same way he always had.
Narrator: The first marker was exactly where it should have been.
Narrator: The second was not.
Narrator: He stopped beside a half-buried stone pillar, worn smooth by centuries of wind, and traced the carved symbol with two fingers. Old work. Older than the outpost. But the direction it pointed was plain enough to read without translation.
Narrator: NEEN-soon came up beside him.
Ninsun: [curious] What is it?
Zik: [flatly] Route marker.
Ninsun: [curious] Does it point toward OO-rook?
Zik: [uneasy] It should.
Narrator: She studied the stone.
Ninsun: [skeptical] Should?
Narrator: Zeek looked at the horizon, then back at the marker. It pointed east. OO-rook was west. Had been west every day of his life.
Narrator: BAH-lahg came up behind them.
Balag: [uncertain] Maybe we read it wrong.
Narrator: Zeek did not answer.
Balag: [reassuring] It happens,
Narrator: BAH-lahg said.
Zik: [flatly] Not to me.
Narrator: En-KHEH-doo, several paces back, smiled.
Enhedu: [amused] There he is.
Narrator: They kept moving.
Narrator: The well should have been less than an hour ahead. Zeek remembered its position the way he remembered debts: the angle of the dunes, the low ridge of black stone to the south, the broken track leading into the basin. He had marked it twice.
[pause 1.5s]
Narrator: They reached the place the map, the sun, and four separate memories agreed it should be.
Narrator: There was nothing there.
Narrator: Flat sand stretched unbroken to the horizon. No depression. No shadow of what had once been a well. The location itself had simply ceased to exist.
Narrator: BAH-lahg turned in a slow circle.
Balag: [uneasy] We're off course.
Ninsun: [firmly] We're not.
Narrator: NEEN-soon had already taken sightings off the sun and the ridgeline.
Ninsun: [uneasy] The desert has stopped agreeing with itself.
[pause 1.5s]
Narrator: They found the well again when the sun stood directly overhead, throwing no shadows at all. It sat several miles off their line of travel, in a place none of them remembered crossing. The stones around its mouth were wet.
Narrator: Zeek stopped at the edge of the basin. Water sat inside, dark and glass-still. He could smell it from ten feet off—iron, old blood, and a taste underneath both that he did not have a word for and did not want one.
Narrator: BAH-lahg took a step toward it.
Zik: [sharply] Don't,
Narrator: Zeek said.
Balag: [defensive] Wasn't going to drink.
Zik: [quietly] Good.
Narrator: Carved fresh into the stone rim, dust still pale and scattered around the cuts, was the spiral-drop symbol. NEEN-soon crouched and held her fingers a hair above the grooves without touching them.
Ninsun: [uneasy] This was made recently. Hours.
Narrator: Nobody had carved it. They had been together every hour since leaving the ridge.
Narrator: Zeek looked down into the black water. Far below the surface—not a reflection, not a trick of light—a shape moved, and it was not still.
Narrator: He stepped back from the edge.
[pause 1.5s]
Narrator: That was when the retreat stopped being a plan.
Narrator: BAH-lahg wanted the staircase. NEEN-soon agreed with him at once.
Ninsun: [urgently] It's the only fixed point we have left. Everything else keeps changing under us.
Zik: [quietly] The staircase changed too,
Narrator: Zeek said.
Zik: [grimly] It just didn't disappear.
Balag: [firmly] It's more than we've got anywhere else out here.
Narrator: BAH-lahg nodded.
Balag: [firmly] We go back.
Narrator: En-KHEH-doo did not move. She stood apart, staring at the empty shimmer of the horizon.
Enhedu: [intense] You keep calling it a prison because your father did,
Narrator: she said.
Narrator: Zeek looked at her.
Zik: [sharply] What?
Enhedu: [intense] Your father. The journal. The warnings. Every time this place scares you, you decide that means it's a trap.
Zik: [flatly] Most things trying to kill us are traps.
Enhedu: [intense] Most things trying to kill us are honest enough to just attack.
Narrator: NEEN-soon frowned.
Ninsun: [skeptical] That makes no sense.
Enhedu: [intense] It makes perfect sense.
Narrator: En-KHEH-doo turned fully toward Zeek. Her eyes had gone bright—not with fear, but with something closer to hunger.
Enhedu: [excited] You keep assuming we need to escape from the First City. What if it isn't?
Zik: [skeptical] What do you think it is, then?
Narrator: She smiled the way a woman smiles when she has already picked her side.
Enhedu: [hungrily] A kingdom.
Narrator: BAH-lahg made a low sound in his throat. NEEN-soon stared at her.
Ninsun: [accusing] You want to go back,
Narrator: NEEN-soon said.
Enhedu: [intense] I want to know what's down there.
Ninsun: [horrified] You want to walk into the place that erased an entire city from history.
Narrator: En-KHEH-doo shrugged.
Enhedu: [casually] You say that like it wasn't impressive.
Narrator: Zeek looked between them. BAH-lahg wanted the staircase because walls still made sense to him. NEEN-soon wanted it because she needed one true thing while the world kept lying. En-KHEH-doo wanted it because she had decided the First City was the biggest score of her life—bigger than money, bigger than anything she had ever put a price on.
Narrator: Zeek wanted it because his father had been there.
Narrator: None of them said their real reason out loud. They turned back anyway.
Narrator: The desert did not make the return easy.
Narrator: They tried to pick up the caravan route that should have carried them to the ridge. Instead they found a road. The wind stripped enough dust away to show paving stones the size of coffins, fitted with a precision that made the outpost ruins look like a child's work.
Narrator: NEEN-soon dropped to her knees at one of the joints.
Ninsun: [awed] This is older than the known dynasties.
Balag: [curious] How old?
Narrator: BAH-lahg asked.
Ninsun: [quietly] I don't know.
Narrator: The answer seemed to cost her more than any number could have.
Narrator: The road ran the wrong direction—away from OO-rook, away from the ridge, toward nothing they recognized. Beside it, pressed into the sand, were footprints. Bare feet. One set. Fresh.
Narrator: The prints started beside the buried road and led toward the ridge. Zeek followed them until they stopped. No second set. No drag marks. No struggle. Only a strip of faded cloth half-buried where the last footprint ended.
[pause 0.5s]
Narrator: Zeek stopped breathing.
Narrator: He knew it before he touched it. The weave. The dye gone the color of old rust. The ghost of smoke and myrrh still clinging to the fibers after twelve years of desert wind that should have scoured it clean.
[pause 0.5s]
Narrator: His father's cloak.
Narrator: He picked it up. For one long moment the desert dropped away and he was eleven years old again, huddled beside a dying fire while his father draped this same cloth over his shoulders.
Bel-iddin: [warmly] [wistful] Desert remembers the sun. Doesn't mean it owes you warmth after dark.
Narrator: NEEN-soon watched him.
Ninsun: [gently] Is it his?
Narrator: He nodded.
Narrator: BAH-lahg looked toward the ridge.
Balag: [quietly] Then he was here.
Zik: [quietly] [bitterly] It wanted me to think he was.
Enhedu: [flatly] Does it matter which?
Narrator: En-KHEH-doo asked.
Enhedu: [coldly] If your father left it, he was here. If something else placed it, then what's out there knows him well enough to make you believe it. Either way, this place stopped just waiting for us a while ago.
Narrator: The wind moved. The footprints vanished between one blink and the next.
Narrator: The cloth was no longer in Zeek's hand.
[pause 1.5s]
Narrator: Nobody spoke. Then BAH-lahg said,
Balag: [firmly] We're going back,
Narrator: and this time no one argued.
[pause 1.5s]
Narrator: It took until nightfall to reach the ridge. The outpost was still there. For a moment Zeek let himself believe that was good news.
Narrator: Then he saw the staircase.
Narrator: It had been twenty feet deep that morning. Now the steps dropped away into a darkness that swallowed torchlight before it traveled more than an arm's length—a darkness with weight to it, like the absence of light had learned to push back.
Narrator: NEEN-soon stood at the edge.
Ninsun: [fearful] That wasn't there.
Balag: [softly] Neither was the rest of this,
Narrator: BAH-lahg said quietly.
Narrator: The wall at the bottom was simply gone. Only the black mouth of the stairwell remained, breathing cold up into the night air.
Narrator: Zeek pulled out the compass. The needle pointed straight down. Then, slow as a held breath, it turned and settled on the darkness of the stairwell itself.
Narrator: The journal opened on its own. Lines formed, wet ink crawling across dry paper.
Narrator: You tried to leave.
Narrator: The mark on his palm woke.
[pause 0.5s]
Narrator: That was wise.
Narrator: NEEN-soon read over his shoulder.
Ninsun: [quietly] That's not comforting.
Zik: [quietly] No.
Narrator: One final line.
Narrator: You should have gone farther.
Narrator: Zeek turned to look back the way they had come.
Narrator: Nothing. Flat sand stretched unbroken in every direction. No footprints. No buried road. No proof any of them had ever stood anywhere but here.
Narrator: BAH-lahg turned a slow circle.
Balag: [fearful] Then how did we get back?
Narrator: Zeek looked down into the stairwell's black throat.
Zik: [quietly] I don't think we did.
Narrator: From somewhere far below came three measured knocks.
Narrator: Three more answered from the open desert behind them.
Narrator: Zeek looked down into the stairwell. Then he turned and looked behind them, at the empty dark where the desert simply stopped being anything at all.
Narrator: There was nothing there.
Narrator: The journal turned a page on its own. A final line bled into the paper, letter by letter.
Narrator: [slowly] YOU ARE ALREADY INSIDE.
[pause 1.2s]
Narrator: The knocking began again—from everywhere at once.
