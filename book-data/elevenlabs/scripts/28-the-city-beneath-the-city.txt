CHAPTER 28 — THE CITY BENEATH THE CITY

Narrator: CHAPTER 28
[pause 1s]
Narrator: The City Beneath the City
[pause 2s]
Narrator: They did not return to the plaza, and the City did not force them to.
Narrator: Instead it simply rearranged the streets until every corridor they chose led back to the same narrow alcove. Zeek noticed the first repetition when he saw the cracked tile under the covered walkway for the second time. BAH-lahg noticed it when the same dripping water resumed its patient count. Ninsun stopped writing after the third return and simply stared at the wall as though the stone itself had insulted her.
Narrator: The man who had refused still sat where they had left him, knees drawn up, humming under his breath. He did not look surprised to see them again.
The Man Who Refused: You tried the eastern passage,
Narrator: he said. It was not a question.
Narrator: BAH-lahg's axe rested across his shoulders.
Balag: We are walking in circles.
The Man Who Refused: No,
Narrator: the man answered.
The Man Who Refused: You are being returned. Circles would be kinder.
Narrator: En-HEH-doo's knife was already in her hand.
Enhedu: How many times does it do this?
Narrator: The man smiled with half his mouth.
The Man Who Refused: Until you stop trying to leave the way you came, or until you decide the price of staying is lower than the price of another attempt. Most people choose the second option before the fifth return.
Narrator: Zeek looked down the narrowing street they had just walked. The geometry had already begun to shift again; the far end no longer matched the memory of the last attempt. The mark under the linen on his palm pulsed once, slow and deliberate, as if confirming a ledger entry.
Narrator: They tried a fourth time anyway.
Narrator: This passage led behind the low wall of inventories and down a slope that grew colder with every step. The air tasted of iron and old paper. For several minutes the City allowed them the illusion of progress. Then the floor leveled, the walls widened, and the same alcove waited at the end of the corridor. The man was still humming.
Narrator: BAH-lahg lowered the axe and sat on the paving stones without ceremony.
Balag: I don't like this place.
The Man Who Refused: That is sensible,
Narrator: the man said.
Balag: I wasn't asking.
The Man Who Refused: The living rarely do.
Narrator: Ninsun had already moved to the long panel of wedge marks that ran along the wall opposite the alcove. She brushed dust from the deepest lines with careful fingers. The torchlight sank into the oily stone and did not return.
Ninsun: These are older than the household inventories,
Narrator: she said.
Ninsun: Different hand. Different purpose.
Narrator: Zeek stepped closer. The mark on his palm had begun to warm in a way that no longer felt like warning. It felt like recognition.
Narrator: Ninsun's stylus hovered above the first line.
Ninsun: The root forms are familiar. Settlement. Binding. Debt. But the combinations refuse ordinary translation. The first line could mean discovered. Or summoned. Those are not the same thing.
Zik: No,
Narrator: Zeek said.
Narrator: She continued, voice growing quieter.
Ninsun: The people who sealed the upper city did not build what lies beneath it. The earliest records claim the first settlers entered from above and found roads, wells, storage chambers already waiting. They believed the place had been abandoned.
Narrator: BAH-lahg looked at the man in the alcove.
Balag: By whom?
Narrator: Ninsun's finger stopped on a character whose edges had eroded.
Ninsun: The word can mean inhabitants. It can also mean remains. Echoes. The dead.
Narrator: The man gave a dry, unused laugh.
The Man Who Refused: I preferred it when we were only lost.
Narrator: En-HEH-doo's eyes stayed on him.
Enhedu: You know this language.
The Man Who Refused: I know what it cost.
Narrator: Ninsun forced herself farther along the wall. The next section was more formal, the wedges deeper and more carefully arranged. Phrases repeated with bureaucratic insistence: the agreement, the binding, the debt below.
Ninsun: Debt is not exact,
Narrator: she said.
Ninsun: Obligation. Price. Something owed to the foundation.
Narrator: Zeek studied the spiral-drop that appeared among the characters.
Zik: You once told me it was a royal emblem.
Ninsun: [flatly] I was wrong.
Narrator: Ninsun's voice had gone flat.
Ninsun: [flatly] It is a claim of ownership. Not chosen. Assigned.
Narrator: The man in the alcove spoke before she could continue.
The Man Who Refused: I know what it means when the mark chooses someone.
Narrator: Zeek turned.
Zik: Then say it.
The Man Who Refused: You would not believe me.
Zik: Try.
Narrator: The man's cloudy eyes held his.
The Man Who Refused: You are already doing what your father did. He believed knowing the truth would make him free of it.
Narrator: The words landed with the weight of an unpaid account. Zeek said nothing. He had spent twelve years carrying the shape of that belief; hearing it spoken by a stranger did not make it lighter.
Narrator: Ninsun reached the final section. The characters here had been cut with force rather than patience. Several surrounding blocks were cracked. She read the first line once, then again, then stopped breathing.
Balag: What does it say?
Narrator: BAH-lahg asked.
Narrator: She looked at Zeek. Whatever he saw in her face drained the last of the anger from his expression.
Ninsun: The First City was not the first city.
[pause 1.5s]
Narrator: Silence settled over the alcove the way dust settles after a collapse.
Narrator: Then the stone moved.
Narrator: A thin groove appeared beneath the final line and carved itself into the black rock without tool or hand. Another line formed beside it. Then another. The City was writing—or correcting.
Narrator: Ninsun translated as the words appeared.
Ninsun: The first city was built over the second.
Narrator: A new line.
Ninsun: The second was built over the third.
Narrator: Her breathing quickened.
Ninsun: The third was built over the—
Narrator: She stopped.
Narrator: Zeek stepped closer.
Zik: Over what?
Narrator: Ninsun stared at the final symbol.
Ninsun: The word is present. But there is no place beneath it. No chamber. No city. No depth. Nothing the language is willing to name.
Narrator: The man in the alcove spoke softly.
The Man Who Refused: Do not ask what lies beneath the last city.
Narrator: Zeek turned.
Zik: Why?
Narrator: For the first time the man's composure cracked. His eyes shifted toward the darkness beyond the alcove, and something that resembled genuine fear moved across the wasted face.
The Man Who Refused: Because it may hear you.
Narrator: The first knock sounded from somewhere beyond the chamber.
Narrator: Three measured strikes. Not loud. Not hurried. The sound traveled through the black stone without echoing, through the soles of their feet and the bones of their bodies.
Narrator: Ninsun turned slowly toward Zeek. He was staring at his left hand.
Narrator: The mark beneath the linen pulsed—not with his heartbeat. The spiral and the drop had darkened until they were almost black. Beneath the symbol the flesh of his palm began to rise. A thin line appeared under the skin, pushing upward from somewhere deeper than muscle or bone.
Narrator: Then the line moved.
Narrator: A single knock sounded against the inside of his palm.
Narrator: The man closed his eyes.
Narrator: Ninsun's voice dropped to a whisper.
Ninsun: [whispers] The knocks are not coming from the walls anymore.
Narrator: Zeek looked at her.
Zik: Then where?
Narrator: She pointed at his hand.
Narrator: The mark knocked again.
Narrator: This time the answer came from far below the City—faint at first, easily mistaken for settling stone or the ordinary shifting of ancient foundations. Then it came again. Three measured strikes.
Narrator: From somewhere beneath them.
Narrator: Zeek's face had gone completely still.
Narrator: The man opened his eyes.
Narrator: And somewhere far below the First City, something knocked back.
[pause 3s]
