Narrator: They started marking the shifted streets on the fifth day.
Narrator: It wasn't Ninsun's idea, though she was the one who made it permanent. After the second uncrossed name and a third alley that closed behind them before they finished measuring it, BAH-lahg simply said,

Narrator: By the second afternoon the chalk marks were gone.
Narrator: Not washed off. Not worn away. Simply absent, as if the wall had never accepted them.
Narrator: en-HEH-doo crouched where the latest mark should have been and ran her thumb across the brick.

Narrator: Ninsun said. She was already pulling a thin bronze stylus and a scrap of harder stone from her satchel.
Ninsun: We need something it can't ignore as easily.

Narrator: BAH-lahg tested the notch with his boot.
Balag: That'll do until it doesn't.
Zik: Same as everything else,
Narrator: Zeek said.
Narrator: They kept the system simple after that. One notch for a passage that had opened and closed. Two notches if it had shown names or objects worth remembering.
Narrator: Three if it had tried to keep them. No one wrote explanations. Explanations were for people who expected the walls to stay still long enough to read them.
Narrator: On the sixth day they found a passage that already carried two notches they didn't remember cutting.
Narrator: No one spoke for a while.
