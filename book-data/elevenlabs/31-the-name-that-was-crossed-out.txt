Narrator: The wall behind the kneeling statue began to open without a sound.
Narrator: White limestone parted along a seam that had not existed a breath earlier. Cold air moved out of the darkness beyond, carrying the same faint scent of ozone and old copper that clung to the ledger chamber. Narrator: They entered.
Narrator: The chamber beyond was larger than the name corridor and colder. The walls here were still white limestone, still covered in rows of personal names, but the cuts were deeper and the crossed-out lines more frequent. Condensation wept down the stone in thin sheets that smelled of a forge just before the metal comes out of the fire, though there was no heat—only cold that behaved as if it remembered being hot.
Narrator: The grain beneath the moisture slid like sand in an hourglass. Looking away and looking back had become its own kind of danger.
Narrator: Zeek found himself staring at a jagged, freshly etched stroke that looked like a scar cut into the stone.
Zik: BAH-lahg,
Narrator: he said. His voice sounded strangely distant even to himself.
Zik: It's your name. It's crossed out.
Narrator: BAH-lahg stepped forward. His face had gone pale. He was not a man who scared easily—Zeek had seen him stand his ground against things with teeth and things with blades and things that were neither—but there was a particular kind of fear that came from being told your own name was written somewhere you could not see it, and that fear had him now.

Narrator: he said, though there was no real conviction behind it.
Balag: There's nothing there but a blur.
Narrator: He leaned closer anyway.

He knew the name. A merchant who sold grain near the Street of Scribes. A man who had once shorted him three measures and laughed when Zeek noticed. An ordinary man under an ordinary sky who, at that very moment, was almost certainly still haggling over prices.
Narrator: It looked less like killing than removing the evidence that anyone had ever been known.
